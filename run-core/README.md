# run-core: the run around the battles

Native C port of PokeRogue's run-level logic: the waves, biomes, the shop's economy, a new battle, a trainer's party and a wild battle's first enemy up to their movesets so far; the enemy moveset next. Like battle-core, it is diff-tested against upstream `pagefaultgames/pokerogue` @ `8555c08`. It reuses battle-core's RNG, V8 `Math.pow`, `js_round` and generated tables (enums, species) **read-only**: nothing in `battle-core/` is changed, and `make` builds battle-core's library into run-core's own build folder.

Experience (`src/exp.c`): `getLevelTotalExp` and `getLevelRelExp` for all six growth rates. Below level 100 upstream reads a table of its own that isn't exported, so `gen/dump-exp.test.ts` dumps its answers for levels 1 to 99 into `gen/out/run_exp.c`; from level 100 the formulas are ported. The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 20000 <seed> exp`) checks every rate at every level from 0 to 3,000 and 20,000 random levels up to 100,000: 138,006 checks, 0 mismatches. 5 of 6 deliberate bugs are caught; the 6th (rounding instead of flooring level³) can't change anything.

Victory experience (`src/exp.c`): `getExpValue` and `applyPartyExp`: who gets experience (not fainted, under the level cap), the wave multiplier, trainer battles' x1.5 and mystery encounters' multiplier, the participants' split and Oval Charm, Exp. Share for the others, Pokerus, Lucky and Golden Eggs in list order (each floors), Exp. Balance's pull towards the split for members at or below the median level. Friendship and Macho Brace, which a defeat also brings, aren't part of it. The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 20000 <seed> partyexp`) builds a real party of 1 to 6 with random levels, fainted members, Pokerus, participants (sometimes one that left the party) and items, and compares the experience phases upstream's `applyPartyExp` queues: seeds 777, 9001 and 4242, 20,000 cases each, 0 mismatches (94,192 awards). 10 of 11 deliberate bugs are caught; the 11th, the median level not floored, can't change anything for whole-number levels. A mystery encounter's own multiplier is ported but not in the test.

A new battle (`src/newbattle.c`, with `gen/out/run_trainers.c` dumped by `gen/dump-trainers.test.ts`: every trainer type's double and gender flags, party templates, its `partyTemplateFunc` captured as wave segments per mode (it has no randomness), and how many names its class has in the English locale, since the name roll moves the RNG): `newBattle` for a new wave in upstream's order: `resetSeed`, trainer or wild, the mystery-encounter check (and its spawn chance going back to the base), the random trainer's type, double roll and variant, the `Trainer` constructor's party-template and name rolls, `checkIsDouble`, then the `Battle` built on the wave seed at offset `wave << 3`, whose `battleSeed` field (16 rolls, the seed of the battle's own RNG) comes before the enemy levels (the trainer's party levels, or one level per wild enemy). A trainer's `partyTemplateFunc` is called while `currentBattle` is still the previous battle, so the port looks it up by the previous wave; with today's templates that can't be told apart from the new wave (the template changes only where the new wave is an X1 wave, which never gets a random trainer). Fixed battles (rivals, gym leaders on fixed waves, evil teams...) report `PR_RUN_UNPORTED_FIXED_BATTLE`: their trainers come from config closures.

The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 10000 <seed> battlegen`, `DIFF_FOCUS=1` for half the waves on gym waves) runs upstream's own `scene.newBattle()` with its dev overrides for battle type, field size, trainers and encounter rate switched off, and compares the battle type, double, trainer type, variant, party template, names, enemy levels, battle seed, spawn chance and RNG state: seeds 777, 9001, 4242 and 31337 (focused), 30,776 covered cases, 0 mismatches (25,835 wild, 3,059 trainer, 1,882 mystery encounter; 6,223 doubles); the 9,224 not covered are daily-run waves and fixed battles. 10 of 12 deliberate bugs are caught, from the battle seed not rolled (30,776 mismatches) to a doubleOnly trainer's partner name rolled (19). The 2 not caught can't change anything: the template func seeing the new wave (above), and a doubleOnly trainer not counted double for its levels (its variant is always DOUBLE).

The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 20000 <seed> trainerspecies`) builds a real `Trainer` of a random type (half with species pools) and variant, an enemy party of 0 to 5 members (real species, random types), a random wave (wave 20 one time in 8) and mode, and calls upstream's own `genNewPartyMemberSpecies` on a freshly sown RNG: seeds 777, 9001, 4242 and 31337, 20,000 cases each, 0 mismatches in species and RNG state (48,912 from species pools, 31,088 from filtered random species). 16 of 18 deliberate bugs are caught, from the evolution step not allowed (43,370 mismatches) to the ULTRA_RARE threshold off by one (12). A trainer's party (`src/trainerparty.c`): `genPartyMember` for each member in turn, as the EncounterPhase calls it. Each member rolls on its own RNG (the run seed at `waveIndex + (derivedType << 10) + ((index + 1) << 8)`, or the static-party and same-seed variants); the species comes from `genNewPartyMemberSpecies` (above), then the double named trainers' split signature pools (Tate's Solrock and Liza's Lunatone first), a same-species template's repeat of its inner template's first member, and the final `getSpeciesForLevel`; then `addEnemyPokemon` up to the moveset, whose form gives the member's types for the next members' balance check. The templates now carry what this reads (`isSameSpecies`, `isBalanced`, the evolution kinds per member and a compound template's offsets, in `gen/out/run_trainers.c`), and the trainer data its seed offset and double partner (`gen/out/run_trainerspecies.c`). A member from `partyMemberFuncs` (closures: gym leaders, the Elite Four, evil teams...; note the key `index - template.size`, which is -1 for the last member) reports `PR_RUN_UNPORTED_TRAINER`, and so do the members after it.

The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 3000 <seed> trainerparty`) builds a real `Trainer` (a quarter of them the double named trainers, half with no `partyMemberFuncs`) on a random run seed, wave (20 one time in 8), biome and mode (every mode: spliced endless fuses every member, the daily run is a plain daily seed), gives the battle the trainer's own `getPartyLevels`, and calls upstream's own `genPartyMember(e)` for every member, with the moveset replaced by a snapshot. Seeds 777, 9001, 4242 and 31337, 3,000 trainers each (6,475 doubles): 0 mismatches in 38,822 members (species and rolls), 10,094 whole parties identical; not covered are the members from `partyMemberFuncs` and the ones after them (7,241, of which upstream's own closure threw on 397 in the test scene). 15 of 18 deliberate bugs are caught, from the member's seed shifted by 9 instead of 8 (9,311 mismatches) to Tate's first member swapped (272) and a same-species template ignored (164). The party's luck (`src/upkeep.c`): `getPartyLuckValue`, what the wild species roll and the reward tiers read. The members allowed in battle add their luck (and their fusion's), plus one for each of a timed event's luck-boosted species; the sum is clamped to 0-14, then the event's luck boost is added, capped at 14. In the daily run it is a roll of 0 to 14 on the run seed instead (a custom daily seed's luck isn't ported). The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 20000 <seed> luck`) calls upstream's own `getPartyLuckValue` on stand-in members that borrow `Pokemon.prototype.getLuck` and `isFusion`, with random luck, fusions, fainted members, event boosts and species, mode, run seed and wave seed: seeds 777, 9001, 4242 and 31337, 20,000 cases each, 0 mismatches (16,165 daily rolls, 4,914 at the cap). 8 of 10 deliberate bugs are caught, from the fusion's luck always counted (38,147 mismatches) to an event species counted twice (1,036) and the daily roll on the wave seed (15,079). The 2 not caught can't change anything: the final `min(..., 14)` makes a clamp at 15 and adding the boost before the clamp give the same results.

An enemy's held items (`src/enemyitems.c`, with `gen/out/run_enemyitems.c` dumped by `gen/dump-enemyitems.test.ts`: the WILD and TRAINER modifier pools by tier, each entry's item, weight and generator, and each trainer type's `isBoss` and whether it has a `genModifiersFunc`): the EncounterPhase's item step. First `regenerateModifierPoolThresholds(enemy field, WILD or TRAINER)`, which asks every generator entry for a type (so the Berry, base stat booster and attack type booster generators roll once each, in pool order) before the thresholds are built from the weights; then `generateEnemyModifiers`: nothing at the classic final boss; per enemy, `ceil(difficulty wave / 10)` chances (x2.5 at a final wave) of 1 in `getEnemyModifierChance`, at least half of them for a boss (a trainer boss makes every member one), and for each item the upgrade roll (1 in 32, halved for a boss, /8 at a final wave), the tier roll of 1024, the pick by weight, and the generator's roll: a berry (Sitrus, Lum, Leppa 2 in 12 each, else one of the other 8), a base stat, or an attack type weighted by the enemy's attack moves (at most 3 per type; none: null, retried from the same tier, a tier down after 100 retries); a Mini Black Hole every 1000th wave. The attack type booster's input (each attack move's types) comes from the moveset, which isn't ported: the caller supplies it. A trainer with a `genModifiersFunc` (a closure) reports `PR_RUN_UNPORTED_TRAINER`.

The test (`tools/difftest.sh <upstream> 3000 <seed> enemyitems`) makes a real enemy party (1 to 6 real Pokemon through `addEnemyPokemon`, with their real movesets, a quarter of them bosses) for a random mode, run seed and wave (up to 3,000, and the 1000th waves now and then), a wild or trainer battle (trainers without a `genModifiersFunc`), single or double; records each enemy's boss flag and the attack type booster's input, asked with the generator's own calls (its six lines transcribed); then runs upstream's own `regenerateModifierPoolThresholds` and `scene.generateEnemyModifiers()` on a freshly sown RNG, recording every item as it is added: seeds 777, 9001, 4242 and 31337, 3,000 cases each, 0 mismatches in 12,000 cases (488,048 items in order, 37,706 attack type boosters, 149 enemies without attack moves, RNG state after). 20 of 23 deliberate bugs are caught, from the generators not asked while building the thresholds (every case) to the booster capped at 2 per type (4) and the Mini Black Hole keyed to the wave instead of the difficulty wave (4); dropping the 100-retry downgrade makes an enemy without attack moves retry forever, which the test catches as a hang. Of the 3 not caught, 2 can't change anything (a zero-weight entry kept among the thresholds, which never wins a pick; the upgrade count kept through a downgrade, which enemies don't read), and the booster with no cap at all needs one type 4 times among an enemy's attack moves, which no case drew.

## Not done (in rough order)

What it reads, for whoever ports it: `getLevelMoves` with evolution, prevolution and relearn moves, egg moves by root species, TM tiers, the balance lists (forbidden, superceded, signature, STAB and off-stat denylists), move attributes (sacrificial, OHKO, weather/terrain setters, charging, stat changes, variable type via `getTypeForMovegen`), abilities with attributes, `calculateEffectivePower` and the Pokemon's stats, and `willTerastallize` for trainers; much of that is battle-core's domain, so it needs coordinating.

The rest of a battle's setup: which mystery encounter spawns; the trainers' `partyMemberFuncs` (closures) and fixed battles' trainers; a wild battle's enemies after the first (they roll on the same RNG as the first one's moveset). `globalScene.randomSpecies` for an empty biome pool (no biome has one today).
3. The reward options: the player's modifier pools and their weights (closures over the party's state) and luck upgrades (`getNewModifierTypeOption`'s player branch; the party luck it reads and the enemy side are ported). Trainers' `genModifiersFunc`.

Dev overrides (`LEVEL_CAP_OVERRIDE`, `ENEMY_HEALTH_SEGMENTS_OVERRIDE`...) aren't modelled; they are upstream's testing switches, off in a real run.

## Layout
- `include/pr/run.h` API, `src/wave.c` the wave functions, `src/biome.c` biomes and encounters, `src/evolution.c` the evolution step, `src/exp.c` experience, `src/upkeep.c` Pokerus, interest and the party's luck, `src/newbattle.c` a new battle, `src/pokemongen.c` a new enemy Pokemon, `src/trainerspecies.c` a trainer's party member species, `src/trainerparty.c` a trainer's party, `src/wildenemy.c` a wild battle's first enemy, `src/enemyitems.c` an enemy's held items and the endless buffs, `src/shop.c` the shop's economy (each function quotes the JS it replaces); `gen/dump-*.test.ts` write `gen/out/run_*.c` (generated, don't edit; `tools/redump.sh <upstream dir>` refreshes them all)
- `tests/*.producer.test.ts` upstream halves of the tests, `tests/*test.c` C halves
- `tools/difftest.sh <upstream dir> [N] [seed] [wave|shop|biome|encounter|newbattle|exp|partyexp|upkeep|battlegen|pokemongen|trainerspecies|trainerparty|wildenemy|luck|enemyitems|enemybuffs|daily|all]` (the upstream checkout comes from `battle-core/tools/setup-upstream.sh`)

Build: `make` (outputs to `build/`).
